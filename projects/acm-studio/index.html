2022–2026

My Journey Through Studio

I entered ACM Studio without knowing how to program. Over the next four years, I learned how to build games, create interfaces, lead teams, teach new members, and help shape the community that first welcomed me.

Fall

2022

The Beginning

Joining Studio With No Programming Experience

I joined ACM Studio during my first quarter at UCLA. At the time, I had no programming knowledge. I simply wanted to find a relaxed club where I could meet other people who loved art, games, and creative projects.

I entered my first game jam, Ludum Dare 53, and designed a cute two-dimensional platformer with my teammates. The game followed a mischievous cat trying to avoid the flash of a camera.

It was my first experience creating something under intense time pressure. We did not finish the game, but I had so much fun that I spent the rest of the year learning functions, loops, conditionals, and the basic structure of programs.

Spring

2023

First Finished Game

Off My Paw-perty

When Ludum Dare 54 arrived, I joined another team as an opportunity to learn new skills and make new friends. I was still not proficient in programming, but I began learning Git, version control, and how to import and implement original sprites in a game.

We reused the same mischievous cat from my first jam, but this time we completed and released a more polished game. Our team won UCLA's Ludum Dare 54 event that quarter.

Spring 2024

Long-Term Development

Villainess Reborn

After participating in several small game jams, I wanted to work on something with enough time to be completed and polished. I joined a team of visual-novel enthusiasts creating a story about a villainess and a heroine.

The game was developed in Godot. I contributed cover artwork, character concepts, sketches, and CG illustrations. By the end of the year, the game had received more than 3,000 downloads on itch.io—not bad at all for a college game.

Fall

2024

From Member to Intern

Designing and Developing the Studio Website

This was the quarter when I decided to become more involved in the organization and applied to be an intern. Every intern had to complete a project before becoming an officer, so I chose to contribute to Studio's website and user-interface design.

I learned Figma while creating the adorable axolotl designs associated with the club. Under the guidance of a senior officer, I also helped implement the ACM Studio website using Next.js, TypeScript, Material UI, and the Notion API.

Notion served as the website's database, but retrieving its content could be slow. My mentor taught me how static generation could improve loading performance. I also learned CSS animation and adapted to TypeScript's strict static type system on top of JavaScript's more dynamic behavior.

Spring

2025

Leadership

Marketing and Engagement Chair

After the interns became officers, the previous chairs prepared to pass their responsibilities to a new generation. Because of my work on the Studio website, I was elected Marketing and Engagement Chair.

We taught twice-weekly workshops covering design, implementation, iteration, and game-production skills. We also organized events and socials for the community.

Serving as chair was rewarding, but it also brought real responsibility. We had to prepare the next intern cohort and teach them the same tools, practices, and lessons that our mentors had once taught us.

2026

Creative Direction

Original Worlds and Fiat Ludum

After our interns became officers and the previous chairs transitioned out of leadership, I had more time to return to game jams and personal creative work.

Together with a talented artist friend and former intern, I developed concept art and worldbuilding for a story I have been writing since I was very young. Although the full game was never implemented, the team meetings were joyful, and the resulting concept artwork was extraordinary.

Fiat Ludum · Best Gameplay

To the Ends of the Earth

We also participated in Fiat Ludum, UCLA's largest game jam. My team created To the Ends of the Earth and won Best Gameplay.

It was one of the most satisfying and fulfilling conclusions possible to my years in Studio: creating something playful alongside people I loved working with, then watching the game succeed in front of the UCLA game-development community.
